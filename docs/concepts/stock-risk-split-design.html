One stock. Two claims. One shared market.

Design exploration · not a live product

Deposit MSFTc once to create a matched Protected and Upside pair. Everyone in the same series holds fungible claims with the same settlement rule. Each user chooses their risk by trading or holding a mix.

01 / LOCKDeposit MSFTcA shared vault holds the underlying stock tokens.

03 / CHOOSETrade exposureKeep either claim, both claims, or a chosen mix.
04 / REDEEMReturn the pairBurn 1 + 1 to withdraw 1 MSFTc before expiry.

Enforceable payout rule

Protected(S) = min(S, max(F, min(S ÷ 2, C))) Upside(S) = S − Protected(S) S = MSFTc value at settlement F = protected target floor C = protected upside cap

Conservation: Protected + Upside always equals the value of the MSFTc locked for the pair. Neither claim can go below zero.

Protection limit: If MSFTc falls below the target floor, the Protected claim receives all remaining backing and still loses value. There is no unconditional dollar guarantee without additional collateral.

This explorer shows settlement payoffs, not fair trading prices today. Before expiry, time, volatility, demand, and market spreads affect each claim’s price.

02 / Fungibility

A later depositor joins the same series

A user’s purchase price does not create a new market. The claim’s contract terms identify the series.

The quote is an editable assumption, not an Othelio valuation or executable offer. Actual bids and asks can differ, and transaction costs create a parity band. Under a standard nonnegative-rate cash valuation, the Protected quote cannot exceed its terminal cap.

Shared-market test: A $100 entrant and a $120 entrant can own identical claims. But a claim with a personal floor tied to each user’s purchase price would pay different amounts to different holders; it cannot be one fungible token.

03 / Market design

One place to choose exposure

Protected

Keep more of the downside buffer

Mint a pair and sell Upside, or purchase Protected directly. Your risk depends on the price you actually pay.

Upside

Take the residual risk

Buy Upside to receive value the Protected holder gives up above its cap and absorb losses first. It can expire worthless.

Neutral

Hold the stock exposure

Hold MSFTc itself or one matched pair. Neutral is a portfolio choice, not a third issued token.

Pricing and liquidity

  1. Use the current executable MSFTc market price as the pair’s trading anchor.
  2. Publish indicative values from a transparent options-style model using term, volatility, and the payoff rule.
  3. Let market makers or an order book post executable bids and asks for each claim.
  4. Route pair minting and redemption through the same interface so arbitrage can discipline the sum of claim prices.

Pair parity constrains Protected + Upside ≈ MSFTc. It does not determine the price of either claim on its own.

04 / Implementation boundaries

What the pilot would need

Contract and accounting

  • Immutable series ID: chain, verified B20 address, expiry, floor, cap, formula version, settlement source.
  • Escrow-backed mint and matched-pair redemption; track raw B20 units and verify outstanding claims never exceed escrow.
  • At expiry, compute each claim’s share fraction from a validated total-return token price; settle using portions of the escrowed B20, with explicit rounding rules.
  • Handle B20 transfer restrictions, pauses, dividends, splits, and issuer multiplier changes without double counting.

Market and user experience

  • One shared trading interface for the active MSFTc series, with visible bids, asks, depth, spread, and an order limit.
  • Show payoff at expiry separately from market price now; display maximum loss from the user’s actual entry price.
  • Settle at a defined market-hours observation; document feed interruption and corporate-action fallback rules before launch.
  • Restrict eligible participants and confirm the issuer permits transfers into the vault and to eventual recipients.
